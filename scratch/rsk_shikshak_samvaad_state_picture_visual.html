🧠 Pedagogical Health & Classroom Practice (Teachers: 23,785) Q95 • Q97 • Q96 • Q82
1. The "Activity Trap" vs. Genuine Student Agency (Q95) 48.1% Misconception
• 48.1% of teachers (11,450) believe active learning means keeping students busy with enjoyable activities without transferring thinking responsibility.
• Only 35.9% (8,548) understand that true agency requires giving students authentic responsibilities based on individual strengths and choices.
2. Classroom Belongingness: Confusion with Praise (Q97) 66.1% Confusion
• Only 33.9% (8,054) recognize that belonging comes from giving students real classroom duties where their impact is felt.
• 66.1% of teachers reduce belonging to oral praise (25.2%), playing games (28.5%), or enforcing identical rules (12.4%).
3. Error Normalization & Shy Student Support (Q96) 62.0% Baseline Mastery
• 62.0% (14,758) correctly treat errors as natural learning milestones without fear.
• 23.0% (5,472 teachers) dumb down questions for shy students (lowering cognitive challenge) instead of providing scaffolded probing.
👁️ Field Delivery & Operational Reality Facilitators & Monitors
1. Severe Monitoring Deficit (Q76 & Q59) 35.1% Unmonitored
• 1,688 clusters (35.1%) had ZERO monitors present during dialogue sessions.
• Where monitors attended (516 clusters), 37.2% noted low teacher talk-time due to facilitator lecturing.
2. Technology Projection Paradox (Q65) 59.1% PPTs Idle
• 71.6% had digital PPTs available on mobile, but in 59.1% of monitored sessions, the PPT was never projected onto a screen due to power/screen unavailability.
3. Module Delivery & Materials (Q71 & Q77) 89.0% Guide Coverage
• 11.0% (529 clusters) lacked physical printed guides (334 soft-copy only, 173 zero guides).
• 728 facilitators requested concept cheat-sheets for complex teacher doubts.
