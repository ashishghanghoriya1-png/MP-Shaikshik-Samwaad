2. What Happens in Classrooms: 4 Key Teaching Habits & Misconceptions Survey Insights

Habit 1: The "Activity Trap" (Q95) • 48.1% of Teachers Trapped
The Question Asked: "What is the main goal of using activities and learning kits in class?"
Response Type What Teachers Said Result & Meaning
The Trap Choice "Keep children busy with lots of activities so they stay active." 48.1% (11,450 teachers) fell into the trap of doing activities without reflection.
The Best Practice "Use activities to help children think, reflect, and understand the concept." 35.9% (8,548 teachers) demonstrated high-level constructivist teaching.
👉 Action: Teach educators that activities are just tools; the real learning happens when students discuss and conclude what the activity taught them.
Habit 2: Building Student Belonging (Q97) • 66.1% Misguided
The Question Asked: "How do you make every student feel they belong in your classroom?"
Response Type What Teachers Said Result & Meaning
The Best Practice "Give students real classroom responsibilities and value their contributions." 33.9% (8,054 teachers) chose authentic student agency.
Common Traps "Play casual games (28.5%) or praise only students who give correct answers (25.2%)." 66.1% (15,731 teachers) relied on superficial games or praise.
👉 Action: Shift teachers from praising only top-performers to making every student feel valued and safe to participate.
Habit 3: How Teachers Handle Mistakes (Q96) • 62.0% Safe & Aligned
• 62.0% of teachers treat mistakes as normal learning steps and encourage shy students to try again without fear.
• However, 23.0% switch immediately to overly simple questions, which lowers classroom rigor.
Habit 4: Discussion Time vs. Facilitator Lecturing (Q98) • 54.2% Discussion
• In 54.2% of sessions, teachers spent most of the time discussing in small groups (meeting the 30:70 talk ratio goal).
• In 45.8% of sessions, facilitators talked too much and drifted into lecturing.
